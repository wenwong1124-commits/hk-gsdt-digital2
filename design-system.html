Clinical field notebook.

Plain ground, plain ink. The only colour on the page arrives inside a photographic cutout.

The system for Wendy Wong — designer in Hong Kong moving into AI product management. The page has one job: prove in sixty seconds that complex AI research becomes scalable digital health products. Four styles, strict hierarchy — Rebus is the voice and now also the entire chroma budget, Utilitarian is the default and 85% of the surface, Bento is the architecture.

00 / before the tokens

Why this palette and these two typefaces

Colour

The interface has no colour. Ground is #FBFAF7 — off-white with a trace of warmth, two steps from pure white rather than the cream that made the earlier draft read as stationery. Everything above it is one of three ink weights or one of two hairline values. That is the whole palette.

Ink is neutral and near-black at #17181A — 17.0:1 on paper, comfortably AAA. Secondary and tertiary land at 6.34:1 and 5.06:1, so every ink weight clears AA for normal text, including the quietest marks on the notebook plate. A portfolio whose central claim is inclusive design for older adults cannot ship a caption that fails contrast.

The green → blue → violet gradient is retired. It was real accumulated equity, but a signature gradient competes with the cutouts for the eye, and in a page where photography is the only chroma it would read as a second, unrelated accent system. The token is left commented in the stylesheet — reinstating it is one line.

Removing hue dissolves the hardest constraint rather than solving it. Metric families were to be told apart by three gradient-derived colours safe for a red-green colour-blind reader; with no hue in the system, they are told apart by a solid, dashed or dotted 3px rule plus their case numeral. Nothing depends on colour perception, because nothing has colour. The same logic runs through status: shipped is filled ink, in trial is a solid hairline, under NDA is a dashed hairline with no fill — three fill treatments, three words, zero hues.

Typefaces

Archivo replaces Inter as the grotesk. Inter is a UI face — its generous x-height and open apertures are tuned for 14px interface text and go soft and slightly bureaucratic at 76px. Archivo comes from a grotesk lineage built for headlines: tighter apertures, a firmer stem-to-counter relationship, and it holds −0.038em tracking at display size without the letters gumming together. Set at 600 it gives the flat, tight, black-on-near-white voice the Rebus reference work runs on.

It also satisfies the constraint that matters most for Rebus: cap-height and baseline are metrically stable across the whole 300–700 range. Pictograms are baseline-locked and optically sized against cap-height, so a face whose caps drift between weights makes every hero line sit wrong. Archivo ships a true italic — drawn, not slanted — down to 300, which the italic subtitle needs.

IBM Plex Mono is the secondary. Tabular figures are structural here, not a nicety — metric blocks and the build log both depend on numerals aligning down a column. Plex Mono is monospaced by construction, so tabular is guaranteed, and it stays legible at 10–11px uppercase with +0.12em tracking, which is most of its work. It reads as instrumentation rather than code-editor, which JetBrains Mono does not.

The known trade-off — a grotesk at display size is colder than a serif — now has only one solution left, which is the correct one: scale, tracking, and the warmth the photographic cutouts bring. No third family, no serif, and no colour to fall back on.

note — one item not delivered literallyasset dependency

Rule 1 of the Rebus system requires photographic cutouts, and photography cannot be authored in code. Every pictogram on this page is therefore an asset slot — correctly sized at cap-height × 1.6, baseline-locked, carrying the right alt text, and visibly a placeholder rather than a vector stand-in. Flat vector or emoji here would violate the rule the system exists to protect, so the slot stays honest and empty until the shot list in §04 is shot. Swapping <span class="pict slot"> for <img class="pict" src alt> changes no layout.

This now carries more weight than it did. With the gradient gone, the cutouts are not an accent among several — they are the only chroma the page has, which makes rule 2 (one consistent key light across the whole library) the difference between a system and a collage.

This paragraph sits at the enforced measure. The token is --prose: 49ch, not 68ch, because 1ch is the advance of the digit zero, which runs far wider than Archivo's average lowercase glyph — so the value was set by measuring rendered lines in the browser, not by arithmetic. Forty-nine ch renders at 66–71 actual characters per line, inside the 65–72 band that protects the sixty-second read.

ratio discipline

85% quiet Utilitarian · 10% Bento structure · 5% loud. Never more than one loud element per viewport — one Rebus line, or one filled stamp. Not two. With no colour in the system, "loud" now means a cutout, a solid ink mass, or display scale, and those are the only three things that can shout.

04 / the system fails fastest here

Rebus rules

Inconsistent cutouts read as clip art, and clip art destroys clinical credibility. Rebus appears in exactly three places: the hero line, section headlines, and one line per project card. Nowhere else — and now it carries all of the page's colour.

  1. Photographic cutouts only. Real objects knocked out of white, hard-edged. Never flat vector, never emoji, never line icons.
  2. Consistent key light, top-left, roughly 35°. Every object in the library lit identically or the set will not cohere. This is a shoot specification, not a post-production note — and with no palette behind it, an inconsistent library has nothing to hide behind.
  3. No shadow on the cutout. A shadow breaks the baseline, and Utilitarian forbids decoration.
  4. Height = cap-height × 1.6, baseline-aligned, small optical side bearing. Archivo cap-height ≈ 0.72em, so --pict-h: 1.15em with --pict-bearing: .12em either side. vertical-align: baseline puts the box bottom on the baseline; no transform nudge.
  5. Maximum three pictograms per sentence. Two is better. Past three the sentence stops being a sentence.
  6. The deletion test. The sentence must still read correctly with every image removed. Pictograms replace nouns only — never verbs, never grammar. Use the toggle at the bottom right of this page to check any line.

Nouns replaced: microphone, signed clinical form. Verbs and grammar untouched. Deletion test — "I turn a microphone into a signed clinical form." Reads. Two pictograms, under the maximum of three.

Accessibility contract

Every pictogram carries alt equal to the exact word it replaces — alt="microphone", not alt="a microphone on a desk". A sentence built from images is hostile to screen readers, so this is mandatory, not advisory. Slots use role="img" with aria-label to behave identically before the photography lands.

06 / reveal content, or confirm an action

Motion

If an animation does neither, it is cut. There is no third category.

tokendurationapplied to
--dur-confirm120ms hover, focus, stamp straighten, border shift
--dur-reveal200ms nav underscore translate, sticky hairline
--dur-enter320ms section and card reveal on first intersection
--ease— cubic-bezier(.2, 0, 0, 1) — fast out, settles late

Reveals fire once, on first intersection, and unobserve. Nothing re-animates on scroll-back — a page that keeps performing has stopped being a document.

absent by decision

  • custom cursor
  • floating decorative shapes
  • parallax
  • scroll-jacking
  • typewriter effects
  • animated gradients — there is no gradient
prefers-reduced-motion: reduce

Every transform is disabled — translate, scale and the stamp rotation. Opacity fades are retained, because they still reveal content without moving anything. Smooth scrolling is switched off in the same query.
