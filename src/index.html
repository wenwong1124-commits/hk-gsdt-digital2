Clinical field notebook.

Pure white, faint ruling, plain ink, handwritten margins. One warm accent, spent only on marks — everything else waits for the photography.

The system for Wendy Wong — designer in Hong Kong moving into AI product management. The page has one job: prove in sixty seconds that complex AI research becomes scalable digital health products. Four styles, strict hierarchy — Rebus is the voice and now also the entire chroma budget, Utilitarian is the default and 85% of the surface, Bento is the architecture.

00 / before the tokens

Why this palette and these two typefaces

Colour

The interface has no colour. Ground is pure white — #FFFFFF — ruled with a 72px grid at 3.8% ink, faint enough to read as paper stock rather than as a drawn line. Everything above it is one of three ink weights or one of two hairline values. That is the whole palette. Two zones stay unruled: the header, and the hero banner — the one line that has to land without anything behind it.

Ink is neutral and near-black at #17181A — 17.8:1 on white, comfortably AAA. Secondary and tertiary land at 6.62:1 and 5.28:1, so every ink weight clears AA for normal text, including the quietest marks on the notebook plate. A portfolio whose central claim is inclusive design for older adults cannot ship a caption that fails contrast.

The green → blue → violet gradient is retired. It was real accumulated equity, but a signature gradient competes with the cutouts for the eye, and in a page where photography is the only chroma it would read as a second, unrelated accent system. The token is left commented in the stylesheet — reinstating it is one line.

One hue is then spent deliberately: a warm terracotta, on vector marks only. It is the same temperature as the wood, skin and paper tones the cutouts will carry, so the marks and the photography read as a single palette instead of competing.

Colour still never carries a distinction alone. Metric families are separated by a solid, dashed or dotted rule and only then tinted — terracotta, blue, plain ink, a trio whose worst simulated separation is ΔE 38 under both deuteranopia and protanopia, against ΔE 5 for the olive-and-terracotta pairing I tested first and discarded. Status works the same way: shipped is filled ink, in trial is an accent hairline, under NDA is a dashed grey hairline with no fill — three fill treatments and three words, so greyscale loses nothing.

Typefaces

Figtree replaces Inter as the primary. Inter is a UI face — tuned for 14px interface text, it goes soft and bureaucratic at display size — and the harder grotesks that fix that (Archivo, Space Grotesk) turn squarish and robotic in running prose. Figtree sits between: a geometric-humanist sans with open, even counters that reads effortless at body size, and even, consistent stroke modulation that stays composed when the hero is set light at 112px.

Display sizes run at 400, not 600. Past roughly 64px the scale already supplies the presence a line needs, and adding stroke weight on top reads as shouting rather than confidence — the slim setting is what keeps a 112px sentence minimal instead of merely large. Headings step to 500 and body stays 400, so weight only ever rises as size falls.

It also satisfies the constraint that matters most for Rebus: cap-height and baseline are metrically stable across the whole 300–700 range. Pictograms are baseline-locked and optically sized against cap-height, so a face whose caps drift between weights makes every hero line sit wrong. Figtree ships a true italic — drawn, not slanted — down to 300, which the italic subtitle needs.

Caveat is the secondary, and it is deliberately starved. Handwriting carrying the whole label layer turned the page into a notebook pastiche — every caption, tag and stamp in a hand that was never going to hold up at 11px with wide tracking. It now appears in exactly three places: the notebook plate, the scroll cue, and the footer wordmark. Measured on the portfolio page itself that is 3.4% of characters and 3.97% of ink — an accent, a signature at the bottom of the page, not a voice that talks over everything.

Which puts the label layer back on Figtree: micro-labels, captions, tags, stamps, metrics, case numbers and log entries, set small and uppercase with wide tracking. Tabular figures survive the move — Figtree's digits are equal-width too, so metric blocks and log dates still align down a column without a monospace. One family does all the work; the other signs it.

note — one item not delivered literallyasset dependency

Rule 1 of the Rebus system requires photographic cutouts, and photography cannot be authored in code. Every pictogram on this page is therefore an asset slot — correctly sized at cap-height × 1.6, baseline-locked, carrying the right alt text, and visibly a placeholder rather than a vector stand-in. Flat vector or emoji here would violate the rule the system exists to protect, so the slot stays honest and empty until the shot list in §04 is shot. Swapping <span class="pict slot"> for <img class="pict" src alt> changes no layout.

This now carries more weight than it did. With the gradient gone, the cutouts are not an accent among several — they are the only chroma the page has, which makes rule 2 (one consistent key light across the whole library) the difference between a system and a collage.

04 / the system fails fastest here

Rebus rules

Inconsistent cutouts read as clip art, and clip art destroys clinical credibility. Rebus appears in exactly three places: the hero line, section headlines, and one line per project card. Nowhere else — and now it carries all of the page's colour.

  1. Photographic cutouts only. Real objects knocked out of white, hard-edged. Never flat vector, never emoji, never line icons.
  2. Consistent key light, top-left, roughly 35°. Every object in the library lit identically or the set will not cohere. This is a shoot specification, not a post-production note — and with no palette behind it, an inconsistent library has nothing to hide behind.
  3. No shadow on the cutout. A shadow breaks the baseline, and Utilitarian forbids decoration.
  4. Height = cap-height × 1.6, baseline-aligned, small optical side bearing. Figtree cap-height ≈ 0.70em, so --pict-h: 1.12em with --pict-bearing: .12em either side. vertical-align: baseline puts the box bottom on the baseline; no transform nudge.
  5. Maximum three pictograms per sentence. Two is better. Past three the sentence stops being a sentence.
  6. The deletion test. The sentence must still read correctly with every image removed. Pictograms replace nouns only — never verbs, never grammar. Use the toggle at the bottom right of this page to check any line.

Nine words: at 112px a hero line has to be short enough to land in one fixation, and every word past about ten is spent on nothing.

Accessibility contract

Every pictogram carries alt equal to the exact word it replaces — alt="microphone", not alt="a microphone on a desk". A sentence built from images is hostile to screen readers, so this is mandatory, not advisory. Slots use role="img" with aria-label to behave identically before the photography lands.

On the live page they sprinkle in from above the fold when the hero is first reached, fall under gravity, collide, and pile above the collage. Bodies are circles — the artwork is roughly as wide as it is tall, so a bounding circle is a close proxy, where the reference used capsules because its labels were long text pills. Spin comes from tangential impulse at each contact, which is what makes them tumble and roll instead of sliding. The loop sleeps after 40 idle frames and wakes on interaction or resize.

This is Wendy's own artwork, not a stand-in — cut from two 3×4 sheets by slice_stickers.py, which floods the magenta ground away from each cell's edges inward, drops any small blob bleeding in from the neighbouring row, then trims and squares the result to 256×256 RGBA. The die-cut white edge is the artwork's own; it reads as invisible against this page's white ground and exists so a sticker survives being placed on any other surface.

Each is inlined as a base64 PNG in an IMAGES map keyed by sticker name, so the page stays self-contained and offline. A drawn SVG fallback remains in the source for any key whose cutout has not been supplied; with all eleven present, none of it renders.

Reveals fire once, on first intersection, and unobserve. Nothing re-animates on scroll-back — a page that keeps performing has stopped being a document. absent by decision

  • custom cursor
  • parallax
  • scroll-jacking
  • typewriter effects
  • animated gradients — there is no gradient

Amended: "floating decorative shapes" was on this list and the sticker field is exactly that. The rule it breaks — every animation must reveal content or confirm an action — is broken knowingly, once, in one place. What it buys is the only thing on the page a visitor can play with, and a portfolio for a product designer that cannot be touched is making an argument it never demonstrates. It stays confined to the hero, sits behind every piece of content, carries no information, and disappears completely under reduced motion.

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce

Every transform is disabled — translate, scale and the stamp rotation. Opacity fades are retained, because they still reveal content without moving anything. Smooth scrolling is switched off in the same query. The sticker field is not frozen mid-air but removed outright: a physics toy with the physics turned off is just clutter, and it carries nothing a reader would miss.
